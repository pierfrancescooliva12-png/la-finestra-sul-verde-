Guide · Siena

Siena in two days,
at a slow pace

Siena is a city to explore on foot: the historic centre is compact, full of slopes, and every alley leads to a square. Two days are enough to see the essentials without rushing, starting from La Finestra sul Verde and entering the city through Porta Pispini, 200 metres from the house.

Day one: the civic heart

  • From Porta Pispini to the Campo: walk along via dei Pispini, then via di Pantaneto and Banchi di Sotto to Piazza del Campo. About 15 minutes on foot.
  • Piazza del Campo and the Fonte Gaia: the great brick shell where the Palio is run. Worth a coffee just to sit and look at it.
  • Palazzo Pubblico and the Museo Civico: home to Ambrogio Lorenzetti's frescoes of Good and Bad Government.
  • Torre del Mangia: over 400 steps for the best view of the city and the hills. Entry is limited, so go early.
  • An afternoon among the contrade: Siena is divided into seventeen contrade, each with its own fountain, colours and museum.
  • Piazza Salimbeni, historic seat of the Monte dei Paschi bank, before dinner.

Day two: the Cathedral and the museums

  • Siena Cathedral: a façade of white and dark green marble, the Piccolomini Library frescoed by Pinturicchio and the inlaid marble floor, fully uncovered only at certain times of year. Check the dates before you go.
  • Museo dell'Opera and the "Facciatone": from the unfinished façade of the New Cathedral there's a spectacular view over Piazza del Campo.
  • Santa Maria della Scala, opposite the Cathedral: a pilgrims' hospital for centuries, now a museum complex.
  • San Domenico and Fontebranda: the places of Saint Catherine, with a fine view of the Cathedral across the valley.
  • Sunset on the terrace: head back for a glass of wine overlooking the valley and the walls.

What to taste

Pici, the hand-rolled pasta, ribollita, cinta senese cured meats and, for dessert, panforte, ricciarelli and cantucci. With the apartment's equipped kitchen you can also shop at the market and cook at home.
