The Palio di Siena,
up close

Twice a year, on 2 July and 16 August, Siena comes to a standstill for the Palio: ten contrade compete in a bareback horse race, three laps around Piazza del Campo. It lasts just over a minute, but the city prepares for it all year.

The Palio days

  • The tratta (29 June and 13 August): horses are drawn by lot and assigned to the contrade.
  • The trial races: six trials, mornings and evenings, over the three days before the Palio.
  • The propitiatory dinner: on the eve of the race each contrada dines outdoors in its own streets.
  • Palio day: in the afternoon the historical pageant parades through the square, followed by the race at sunset.

Practical tips

  • Book well ahead: accommodation in town sells out months before the Palio.
  • Get around on foot: the centre is closed to traffic and crowded. From La Finestra sul Verde, Piazza del Campo is about a 15-minute walk through Porta Pispini.
  • To watch the race you can stand in the centre of the square for free, arriving many hours early, or buy a seat in the stands around the track.
  • Bring water and a hat: in July and August there's a long wait in the sun.
  • Leave the car in the free parking in the streets near the house until the celebrations are over.

After the race

Head back for dinner on the terrace, overlooking the valley and the city walls. It's worth staying an extra day: the winning contrada celebrates in the streets and the city is quieter.
